## What is Caprae’s Mission?

Caprae’s mission is not to run a conventional PE fund that wins on cheaper leverage. It is to be a founder-operator platform that helps acquisition entrepreneurs actually close and then *operate* lower-middle-market businesses, using software, process, and people instead of financial engineering as the main wedge.

That shows up in how the firm describes itself: Search-as-a-Service, M&A as a seven-year post-close journey, AI-readiness for portfolio companies, and products like SaaSquatch that compress sourcing from a two-year MBA search into a repeatable pipeline. Kevin’s own framing is horsepower over mileage — find people who can build, then give them a system. The mission is to change who gets to be a buyer in the ETA market, and how much of the value is created after the LOI rather than at the closing dinner.

I read that as an infrastructure mission. Searchers fail on time, cost, and lonely execution. Caprae’s bet is that a tech-enabled operating system — sourcing, outreach, diligence support, and post-acquisition AI — can make independent sponsorship and self-funded search less of a graveyard and more of a craft.

If that mission is real, the full-stack work is not “make a prettier dashboard.” It is to encode judgment: which owners are worth a call, which records are junk, and how a junior associate can still sound like someone who has been in the seat.

## Why do you want to work at Caprae Capital?

I want to work where the product and the P&L are the same object. Most “fintech” roles hide you behind a roadmap that never talks to a business owner. Caprae’s stack sits on the actual search: lists, calls, meetings, LOIs. That is the work I want to be measured on.

I also want a place that selects for stamina and independent thinking instead of pedigree theater. The handbook is explicit about that, and the culture writing around speed of communication and #BleedandBuild is consistent with it. I would rather be in a room that ships, writes in public threads, and expects people to own outcomes than in a room that optimizes for looking senior.

The assignment itself is why I’m applying to *this* seat. SaaSquatch is already good at filling a hopper. The gap I chose to build is the gap a searcher feels at 4 p.m.: ten hours of calling capacity, a thousand rows, and no honest ranking. I care about that class of problem — turning messy go-to-market data into a decision — more than I care about a generic React shop.

I need visa sponsorship. I am saying that plainly because hiding it wastes everyone’s time. I can start immediately, I can work the training hours, and I am not treating Caprae as a stamp. I want a career home where I can get unusually good at building the operating layer of lower-middle-market M&A.

## How is Caprae Changing the ETA Space and Broader PE?

Traditional search is structurally strained. Too many MBA searchers chase the same $5–15M EBITDA stories, LPs want larger checks to cover overhead, and a lot of “search” is still a spreadsheet plus LinkedIn plus hope. Self-funded and independent sponsor paths are absorbing energy because they are more operator-native. Caprae is building for that shift instead of defending the 1980s search-fund brochure.

The change is unbundling the search CEO from having to be a full sourcing shop, a full call center, a full diligence team, and a full post-close operator on day one. Search-as-a-Service, white-labeled outreach, and SaaSquatch are how you industrialize the top of funnel without pretending every searcher should hire a ten-person in-house team at PE prices. Pricing 30–60% below traditional advisors matters only if the *system* is better: more owner meetings, cleaner lists, faster iteration.

On the broader PE side, Caprae’s “founder/operator first” line is a critique of financiers who have never run a plant, a clinic, or a route-based services company. AI-readiness as a post-acquisition product is the logical second move: the multiple is not created at purchase; it is created when a boringly good business gets better processes, better data, and a team that can actually use the tools. That is MaaS as a seven-year hold mindset, not a CIM flip.

The cultural pieces are part of the strategy, not decoration. Speed of communication, no-DM for work, write-then-meet — those are how a distributed team serving searchers in multiple countries avoids becoming another slow professional-services firm. If ETA is going to be rebuilt, it will be rebuilt by people who treat operations and messaging as seriously as they treat a CIM.
